# Punk Hair And Scene-Atmosphere Research

Status: active visual research
Date: 2026-07-24
Scope: UPRISE listener-avatar identity presets

## Purpose

Build a source-backed visual vocabulary for Punk avatar packs. Hair, facial
expression, clothing, and accessories should make each character feel like a
person from a living scene rather than a generic punk costume.

This research informs original UPRISE illustrations. Documentary photographs,
archives, oral histories, and ephemera are references only and must not be
traced or copied.

## Founder Direction

- Research Punk hairstyles much more deeply before continuing the catalog.
- Produce presentation groups in this order:
  1. male-presenting;
  2. female-presenting;
  3. nonbinary.
- Include the devilock associated with the Misfits/horror-punk tradition.
- Include liberty spikes, bihawks, trihawks, and a rat-tail construction.
- Preserve the approved R3 balance: stylized and expressive, but neither
  overly detailed nor bland.

## Research Findings

### Punk Hair Is Not One Timeline

- Early 1976-1978 British Punk is strongly associated with short, chopped,
  bleached, uneven, or hand-spiked hair. Giant Mohicans should not be projected
  backward as the default 1977 look.
- Taller mohawks, segmented hawks, and liberty-spike constructions become
  stronger later and overlap with street-punk and charged-hair traditions.
- North American hardcore often rejected an elaborate fashion uniform. Crew
  cuts, close crops, shaved heads, ordinary short hair, and practical styles
  belong beside more visibly constructed punk hair.
- Horror punk contributes the devilock: shorter sides/back with a long front
  section combed forward. It should appear as one authored identity, not as the
  default horror treatment for every character.
- Deathrock/post-punk contributes wider teased or backcombed center shapes,
  long asymmetry, black dye, and more theatrical silhouette.
- Psychobilly crosses punk with rockabilly through high quiffs, pompadour
  structures, shaved sides, and exaggerated front volume.
- Skate and regional DIY scenes often use practical shags, grown-out crops,
  caps or beanies, rough mullets, and styles that can survive movement rather
  than only staged portraiture.
- Crust and gutter references may include long hair, locs, rough braids,
  shaved sections, and uneven self-maintenance. Do not turn poverty,
  homelessness, dirt, or addiction into costume shorthand.
- Crust packs should not use one loc/dreadlock silhouette as a universal token.
  Research and author multiple forms: short and long locs, thin and thick
  sections, tied-back or loose arrangements, partial undercuts, mixed lengths,
  and different maintenance states.
- Exposed-scalp styles should support optional tattoo overlays without making
  a tattoo permanent to the underlying head identity.
- Black Punk identities need authored natural-texture choices: close coils,
  shaped curls, locs, braids, fades, shaved patterns, and mixed constructions.
  Do not treat one texture as the only Black hairstyle or use culture as a
  detachable prop.

### Scene Atmosphere Comes From Participation

- Documentary archives repeatedly center fans, venue workers, zine makers,
  photographers, record-store workers, radio hosts, promoters, artists, and
  the people doing behind-the-scenes labor, not only famous musicians.
- Punk material culture includes homemade records, xeroxed zines, flyers,
  stencils, hand-lettering, stickers, badges, altered shirts, patched jackets,
  ticket stubs, set lists, and venue histories.
- A genuine scene does not look fully coordinated. Hair dye can show roots,
  cuts can be uneven, repair can remain visible, and one person may wear a
  plain work shirt beside someone with an elaborate charged hairstyle.
- DIY is not simply a distressed texture. It is the visible result of people
  producing, distributing, repairing, booking, broadcasting, and documenting
  their own culture.
- Regional scenes should inherit local venue, weather, workwear, skate, art,
  and music crossover without becoming different product architectures.

### Austin-Specific Atmosphere Reference

- Austin Punk developed through local infrastructure such as Raul's, Inner
  Sanctum Records, local radio, homemade records, film, flyers, and a network
  of people willing to play or promote material outside established channels.
- Raul's was a small Tejano bar before becoming central to the emerging scene.
  That history argues against treating Austin Punk as a London costume copied
  into Texas.
- Later Austin underground history includes no-frills venues, all-ages and
  no-cover practices, Red River spaces, skate/art crossover, queer spaces,
  noise, metal, post-punk, and other adjacent scenes.
- Visual atmosphere should therefore mix heat, practical clothing, faded
  color, local hand-printing, skate/workwear, handmade display, and highly
  individual styling.

## Expression Distribution

Use all 24 expressions across the pack rather than defaulting to neutral:

- dry amusement;
- tired but alert;
- open laughter;
- skeptical side-eye;
- defiant;
- relaxed;
- irritated;
- devious grin;
- quiet confidence;
- soft smile;
- focused;
- playful;
- bored;
- intense;
- friendly;
- surprised;
- unimpressed;
- joyful;
- deadpan;
- mischievous.
- wary;
- delighted;
- stoic;
- crooked grin.

Expression is independent of hairstyle and subcommunity. Do not assign one
personality to an entire scene.

## Production Translation

- Each approved identity preset combines head, face, expression, and hairstyle.
- Face, ears, and neck use one clean skin mask so the listener's selected skin
  tone can be applied separately.
- Every preset uses the same neck width, centerline, and lower cut.
- Hair and facial line art remain distinct from the skin mask.
- Hair color uses a separate palette/mask when the geometry supports it; do not
  create full duplicate heads only to change hair color.
- Hair length, volume, thickness, and arrangement changes remain separate
  authored geometry variants because they alter silhouette and compatibility.
- Scalp tattoos remain optional overlays registered to the standard head and
  are previewed only with hairstyles that leave the relevant area visible.
- Initial garments remain separate:
  - undershirt/top;
  - open outerwear/strap layer.
- Do not create tone duplicates for each identity.

## Drift Risks

- A sheet dominated by mohawks and liberty spikes.
- Treating 1977, hardcore, crust, horror punk, and deathrock as one period.
- Repeating one face and changing only hair.
- Making every haircut salon-perfect, symmetrical, or freshly dyed.
- Assigning race, politics, class, sexuality, addiction, or personality from a
  hairstyle.
- Copying a famous musician's face, logo, or exact stage image.
- Turning local atmosphere into a costume without venue, print, radio, work,
  skate, art, and participation context.
