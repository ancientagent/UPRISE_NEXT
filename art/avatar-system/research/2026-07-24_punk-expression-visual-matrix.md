# Punk Expression Visual Matrix

Status: research basis for character-catalog exploration
Date: 2026-07-24
Scope: crust, gutter, street, 1977, anarcho, skate, and 1980s day-glo punk

This matrix supports one bounded fourteen-character review sheet containing two
visually distinct expressions of each requested punk tradition. It is visual
research, not a rule that every person in a scene dresses this way.

## Research Boundary

- Use historical and documentary references to distinguish garment vocabulary,
  layering, silhouette, color, wear, and DIY treatment.
- Do not copy an identifiable person's likeness, exact outfit, real band logo,
  copyrighted patch, or photographer's composition.
- Do not turn scene vocabulary into demographic, political, cleanliness, or
  personality caricature.
- Allow overlap between scenes; punk style is self-authored rather than a
  uniform.

## Expression Matrix

| Expression | Core silhouette and layers | Surface treatment and color | Hair and details | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crust punk | Workwear, black denim or military-surplus jacket/vest, hoodie or torn tee, dense practical layering | Hand-sewn patches, visible repairs, oxidized studs, camouflage/olive/black with dirty white and one weathered color | Dreadlocks, rough braids, shaved sections, messy long hair, found-object or hemp details | Clean fashion battle-jacket styling; treating poverty as costume |
| Gutter punk | Scavenged and layered street/work clothing, oversized surplus coat, flannel, sweater, bandana, backpack straps | Mismatched repairs, paint wear, patched canvas, faded earth/black colors, improvised closures | Beanie, cropped or unkempt hair, scarf, utility cord, small found accessories | Making it simply "crust with a mohawk"; glamorizing homelessness or addiction |
| Street punk | Leather/denim biker jacket or vest, tapered/torn layers, tartan or military influence | Bright painted leather, studs, spikes, chains, patches, bleach, red/cobalt/yellow/white accents | Mohawk, liberty spikes, charged hair, shaved styles, visible piercings | Giving every punk the same black studded jacket and mohawk |
| 1977 punk | Lean leather-jacket, blazer, torn tee, narrow shirt/tie, straight or tight silhouette | Rips, safety pins, zips, handwritten marks, black/white with red or fluorescent pink; less surface density than later street punk | Short messy spikes, cropped bleach, shag, asymmetry, simple chain or choker | Later-era full battle-jacket overload; giant liberty spikes as the default |
| Anarcho punk | Black or charcoal military/work jacket, narrow trousers, cloth or imitation-leather layers | Monochrome stencil language, political/peace abstraction, white-on-black patches, restrained olive/gray; functional construction | Cropped, shaved, simple mohawk, natural messy hair; minimal decoration outside messages | Copying real movement logos; treating it as maximal street punk |
| Skate punk | Practical hoodie, tee, open flannel/work shirt, coach or work jacket, loose movement-oriented silhouette | Worn graphics, simple patches, checker or stripe accents, faded black/navy/burgundy/khaki | Short hair, buzzcut, shag, beanie or cap; minimal jewelry | Heavy studs/spikes that erase the functional skate silhouette |
| 1980s day-glo punk | Punk leather, denim, knit, or improvised layers treated as a bright personal canvas | Fluorescent lime, magenta, cyan, orange, painted panels, stripes, splatter, marker graphics, contrasting laces and trims | Charged or asymmetric bright hair, graphic makeup, synthetic color accents | Turning it into generic new wave, cyberpunk, rave wear, or one neon mohawk on an otherwise generic jacket |

## Facial Expression Vocabulary

The whole-character catalog may treat facial expression as part of an authored
identity. Do not repeat one blank face across the sheet. The fourteen-character
study should include a balanced range such as:

- open or restrained smile;
- laugh or broad grin;
- frown;
- stern focus;
- tired or weathered look;
- skeptical side-eye;
- devious or mischievous smirk;
- surprised or energized look;
- deliberately woozy or drunk expression.

Do not assign one expression to an entire punk subcommunity or imply that a
scene determines behavior. Avoid grotesque intoxication, injury, or degrading
caricature.

## Source Notes

- Museum at FIT's 1977 punk-fashion history connects the foundational look to
  greaser leather, tight jeans, work boots, distressing, studs, rips, and safety
  pins:
  `https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/1977-westwood-mclaren-god-save/`
- British Culture Archive's Janette Beckman and People's Archive collections
  document the breadth of late-1970s and early-1980s punk fans rather than only
  later stereotypical mohawk/leather looks:
  `https://britishculturearchive.co.uk/janette-beckman-subculture-mods-rudeboys-punks/`
  and `https://britishculturearchive.co.uk/the-peoples-archive-punk/`
- The American River Current train-hopping feature was used only to study
  practical rail-rider layering and carried gear:
  `https://www.arcurrent.com/magazine-dam/2015/05/04/rail-riders-inside-the-invisible-culture-of-train-hopping/`
- Smithsonian's review of Ed Templeton's `Wires Crossed` supports the 1990s to
  early-2000s DIY skate context:
  `https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/the-ten-best-photography-books-of-2023-180983345/`
- Dazed's Poly Styrene style history supports bright, inexpensive,
  self-authored color as a punk alternative to uniform black leather and studs:
  `https://www.dazeddigital.com/fashion/article/43847/1/poly-styrene-dayglo-xray-spex-celeste-bell-style-riot-grrrl-punk`
- Dudley People's Archive photographs were used to study early-1980s painted,
  tartan, studded, and brightly styled street-punk variation:
  `https://www.dudleypeoplesarchive.com/stories/arthur-harris-meets-the-young-punks`
- Public image search was used to compare painted leather, military jackets,
  repairs, studs, patches, layering, and skate silhouettes. Search results were
  not downloaded or reproduced.

## Later Expression Packs

The parent Punk community should not stop at these seven. Candidate later
research packs include hardcore, peace punk, folk punk, post-punk/deathrock,
psychobilly, ska punk, riot grrrl, queer punk, garage punk, glam punk, and
regional or city-specific scene expressions. Each requires its own source-backed
study before character production.
