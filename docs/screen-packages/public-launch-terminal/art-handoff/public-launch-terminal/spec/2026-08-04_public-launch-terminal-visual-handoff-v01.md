## What This Visual Target Establishes

- A worn physical laptop is a desktop-only presentation frame for the public
  terminal; it is not a literal product device or a required mobile container.
- Xerox black/charcoal texture, muted amber/olive terminal type, thin rules,
  and restrained physical wear establish the public launch visual language.
- A compact top navigation makes `Dispatches`, `Blog`, `System`, and `Contact`
  visible peers. `Dispatches` is the selected first section.
- Dispatch Index is a central content section for team-published community
  updates, not the whole site and not an in-app user Feed.
- The selected dispatch can reveal a short excerpt, narrated transmission
  treatment, and transcript affordance.
- The side rails carry UPRISE identity, short mission/status context, current
  section explanation, a visually reserved email-updates field, and public
  contact context.

## Visual Rules To Preserve

- Readable public archive first; atmosphere comes from texture and type, not
  misleading product claims.
- Tone is independent/underground and compact, not polished corporate SaaS,
  neon cyberpunk, or an invented clandestine/security system.
- Amber/olive is an accent and legibility layer, not a glowing signal effect.
- Use no city-specific fixture, real person, real venue, or fabricated live
  community status in the first target.
- The desktop bezel must disappear on mobile rather than shrinking into an
  unreadable device illustration.
- The terminal can be reached directly; tower descent is a later entry motion
  sequence and must not be hard-wired to this initial public screen.

## Design-Only / Open Behavior

The candidate image shows controls/labels that are visual direction only until
the UX plan's open questions are resolved:

- content and navigation model for Dispatches, Blog, System, and Contact;
- dispatch source, review/publish workflow, timestamps, and list pagination;
- narrated-audio source, rights, hosting, transcript, and player behavior;
- email storage, consent, provider, delivery, unsubscribe, and error handling;
- contact endpoint and whether it is a link, form, or external destination;
- entry-door location and relation to conventional account onboarding.
