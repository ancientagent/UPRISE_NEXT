## Clarifications

- The current preferred character models remain liked; the issue is garment
  construction, not a rejection of the character style.
- Outerwear must provide enough quiet surface area for a readable patch.
- Existing UPRISE avatar studies already demonstrate better patch-hosting
  proportions and should be reused as construction references.
- P03 improves the model and patch-panel direction.
- The next character sheet should include more varied spikes and related Punk
  detailing.
- Character-sheet previews do not need empty outlined patch placeholders.
  Installed sample band-name or logo patches can demonstrate usable scale.
- Patch names and detailed artwork are expected to become readable when the
  avatar is enlarged and centered in the expanded top shell, not in compact
  avatar placements.

## Feature Sets

- No new runtime feature is activated.
- This clarification changes outerwear construction and asset QA.

## Working Interpretation

- Keep the approved R3 character language while borrowing successful garment
  panel geometry from retained outfit studies.
- Current construction references include:
  - `male-bust-outfit-study-r1` Vest 01;
  - `male-bust-outfit-study-r2` Patched Vest 01;
  - `male-bust-outfit-study-r2` Folk Overshirt 01.
- Reserve one primary outerwear patch panel separately from smaller
  pin/button zones.
- In review renders, place a separate sample patch on that panel using a short
  fictional UPRISE band name or owned logo mark.
- Preserve the open undershirt print area rather than solving patch space by
  closing the jacket.
- In compact avatar crops, preserve the patch's recognizable mark. Treat the
  enlarged centered avatar in the expanded top shell as the primary patch-text
  readability target; profile and Personal Space views may also expose it.
